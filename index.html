Harvest Now, Decrypt Later

The breach has already happened. We just cannot read it yet.

The attack is storage, not computation

An adversary does not need a quantum computer today. They need only to record your encrypted traffic and exfiltrated data now, then decrypt the archive once a cryptographically relevant quantum computer exists. RSA, ECDSA, Diffie-Hellman and DSA all fall to Shor's algorithm.

The real deadline is your data's lifetime

What matters is not when the quantum computer arrives, but how long your data must stay secret. A system with a 25-year confidentiality requirement is already compromised if its traffic was captured yesterday. Health records, financial data, government communications and trade secrets are all in scope today.

The exposure grows fastest in AI-written code

Code assistants pick cryptographic primitives by pattern completion - RSA-2048, default JWT signing algorithms, MD5, deprecated TLS settings. It compiles, it passes tests, and it is quantum-vulnerable. Vibe-coded applications expand the attack surface faster than security teams can review it.

The Deadline Is Already Set

This is not a speculative timeline. Every major standards body and government has published dated migration requirements.

  1. Aug 2024

    NIST finalizes the post-quantum standards

    FIPS 203 (ML-KEM) for key establishment, FIPS 204 (ML-DSA) and FIPS 205 (SLH-DSA) for digital signatures. The replacement algorithms now exist.

  2. Nov 2024

    NIST IR 8547 sets the deprecation clock

    RSA-2048 and P-256 are deprecated for new systems by 2030, and quantum-vulnerable algorithms are disallowed in NIST standards by 2035.

  3. 22 Jun 2026

    Executive Order 14412 names the threat

    The order describes adversaries "collecting United States information now, and decrypting it later once large-scale quantum computers are operational" - harvest now, decrypt later, stated as federal policy. It sets 31 Dec 2030 for PQC key establishment and 31 Dec 2031 for digital signatures on high-value and high-impact systems.

  4. 24 Jun 2026

    OMB M-26-15 mandates a central CBOM

    A five-phase federal migration running 2026 to 2035, requiring agencies to build cryptographic inventories and populate a central Cryptographic Bill of Materials. Agency migration plans are due 22 October 2026.

  5. ~Mar 2027

    CBOM minimum elements published

    EO 14412 gives CISA and NIST 270 days to define the minimum elements of a Cryptographic Bill of Materials. Quantsiv already emits CycloneDX 1.6 CBOMs, so alignment is a mapping exercise rather than a rebuild.

  6. 2030 – 2035

    Compliance becomes mandatory

    Key establishment by end-2030, signatures by end-2031, and full migration by 2035. National Security Systems follow NSA CNSA 2.0 to a 2033 deadline, and contractors are pulled in through federal acquisition rules.

Sources: EO 14412, OMB M-26-15, NIST IR 8547, CISA Post-Quantum Cryptography Initiative, NSA CNSA 2.0.

How Quantsiv Works

From code scanning to an audit-ready CBOM in 90 seconds

1. Discover

Quantsiv scans your repositories for cryptographic usage - source code, dependencies, configuration files, certificates and live TLS endpoints - and builds a complete inventory of your cryptographic assets. Agentless, installed through a GitHub App or Action, with no changes to your runtime.

2. Analyze

Each finding is evaluated against the quantum-vulnerable algorithms - RSA, ECDSA, Diffie-Hellman, DSA - and scored on two axes: cryptographic severity, and harvest-now-decrypt-later exposure, meaning how long the protected data must stay secret against the projected arrival of a quantum computer.

3. Prioritize

Results are ranked so remediation follows business risk rather than raw finding count. A 30-year health record and a short-lived session token are not the same exposure, even when both use RSA. Every finding carries a plain-English explanation and its recommended replacement.

4. Prove

Export a CycloneDX 1.6 Cryptographic Bill of Materials and an audit-ready compliance report citing the authority behind each requirement. Continuous monitoring flags new quantum-vulnerable code the moment it is introduced, so the migration does not regress.
